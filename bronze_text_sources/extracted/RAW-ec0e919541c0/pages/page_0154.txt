On Canopus

[Agastya or the star Canopus has received considerable
attention in India since Vedic times. Agastya was originally a
sage known from the Rgveda, who after his time got identified
with the southern star, Canopus. Here we cannot go into the
analysis of Vedic hymns that refer to the star. The | 2" Chapter
in BS is titled Agastya-cara that is movement of Agastya. One
wonders why Varahamihira in BS should have captioned this
chapter like his previous chapters on planets which wander in
the sky. We have seen in PT (1.10) how a student of Parasara
doubts whether Agastya could be a graha (seizer or planet). A
recognizable character needed for an object to be taken as a
== was its conjuction with other celestial objects due to its
movement and occasional invisibility. Agastya appears to satisfy
the second condition, though it does not conjunct with any other
star or planet. It is likely the first sky watchers in ancient India

suspected Agastya to be a planet before understanding its true
nature as a star. ]
