Fig. 3.35 Determination of the kālalagna when the Sun lies in the third and the fourth quadrants.



When the Sun is in the third quadrant, as shown in the Fig. 3.35(a), it is clear that
the time taken by it to rise after Γ ′ is θ + ∆ α . Therefore the time taken by S to rise
after Γ is
                                 L′ = 180 + (θ + ∆ α ).                        (3.295a)
When the Sun is in the fourth quadrant, the time taken by Γ to rise after the Sun is
= θ − ∆ α (refer Fig. 3.35(b)). Hence, the time taken by S to rise after Γ is

                                                               L′ = 360 − (θ − ∆ α ).                                                                 (3.295b)

 In the above, the kālalagna is the time difference between the sunrise and the rise
of Γ . From now onwards, the kālalagna or kāla is used to denote the time interval
between the desired instant and the rise of Γ . Then, the kāla at any time

                                              L′′ = L′ + prān.as elapsed after sunrise,                                                                (3.296)

which is the time after the rising of Γ , or the time measured from Γ .
