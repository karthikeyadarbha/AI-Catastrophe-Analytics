      The bhujājyā and kot.ijyā of the difference between the longitudes of the Sun and the
      Moon is multiplied by half of the kot.ijyā of the difference between the longitudes of the
      Sun and the mandocca of the Moon and divided by the trijyā. Of these two, the one
      obtained from the kot.ijyā is applied to 10 times the hypotenuse of the Moon in minutes,
      positively or negatively depending on whether the ayanas are the same or different. The
      square root of the sum of the squares of that and the bāhuphala is the distance of separation
      between the Moon and the Earth (in yojanas). Whatever is obtained from the bhujājyā [or
      bāhuphala] has to be multiplied by trijyā and divided by that [dvitı̄ya-sphut.a-karn    . a].
      The result obtained must be applied positively or negatively to the true (manda-corrected)
      Moon, depending upon whether the longitude of Sun minus the mandocca of the Moon
      lies within the six signs beginning with Karka or Mr.ga in a bright fortnight, and reversely
      in a dark fortnight.
