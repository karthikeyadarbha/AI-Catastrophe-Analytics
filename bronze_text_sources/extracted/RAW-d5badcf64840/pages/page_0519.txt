Fig. D.3 The is..ta-digvr.tta passing through a planet.


  In Fig. D.3, X is the planet whose declination is δ . Let H be the hour angle. Since
PX = 90 − δ , the distance between X and the north–south circle will be

                                     = R sin H sin(90 − δ )
                                     = R sin H cos δ .                                          (D.24)

But the maximum angle between the north–south circle and the is..ta-digvr.tta on
which X is situated at a distance z from the zenith is 90 − a. Therefore the distance
between X and the north–south circle is also

                                  = R sin z sin(90 − a)
                                  = R sin z cos a = chāyā-koti.                               (D.25)

Equating the two expressions, we get

                         R sin H cos δ = R sin z cos a = chāyā-koti.
