In the following we show that the expression given by Nı̄lakan.t.ha is exactly the
same as the above equation.
   For this, we initially need to find out θ as per the procedure in the text. If d
represents the half-duration of the day, ∆ α the ascensional difference, tr the time
elapsed since sunrise, and ts the time that is yet to elapse till sunset, then

                           θ = tr − ∆ α        (forenoon)                      (3.53)
                           θ = ts − ∆ α        (afternoon).                    (3.54)

∆ α is positive when the Sun has a northern declination and negative otherwise.
Hence the magnitude of ∆ α has to be subtracted from tr or ts for northern declina-
tions, and added for southern declinations.

Considering the spherical triangle, PZS′ , where PZ = 90◦ − φ , ZS′ = 90◦ and Z P̂S′ =
Ht (the hour angle of the Sun at the sunset), it follows from the cosine formula that

                                cos Ht = − tan φ tan δ .                       (3.57)

Using the fact that Ht = 90 + ∆ α , the above equation reduces to

                                sin ∆ α = tan φ tan δ .                        (3.58)

Substituting for sin ∆ α in (3.56), we see that it reduces to (3.52), showing that the
expression given by Nı̄lakan.t.ha for mahāśaṅku is nothing but the standard spheri-
cal astronomical result for the Rcosine of the zenith distance of the Sun at any time
during the day.
    The mahācchāyā is defined as the square root of the difference between the
squares of the trijyā and the mahāśaṅku. Since the mahāśaṅku is R cosz, it is
