     Now we discuss the signs as enunciated in the text. Consider the situation when
the declination is northerly (δ positive), as shown in Fig. 3.30(a). Then the first term
in the numerator is equal to | sin δ | cos z and is positive. For a northerly declination,
the Sun can be south or north of the prime vertical. Then we have to take the ‘−’
sign in (3.218), when the āśāgrā is north (Sun at S′ ) and the ‘+’ sign when the
āśāgrā is south (Sun at S).
     In the latter case, H < 90◦ and cos H is positive, and we have to take the sum
of the magnitudes of the two terms. In the former case, the second term is negative
when H < 90◦ , so that x will be the difference of the magnitudes of the two terms.
However, the second term is positive when H > 90◦ , and we have to add the mag-
nitudes of the two terms. This corresponds to the situation when the Sun is between
the unman. .dala and the horizon, as at S′′ .
     When the declination is south (δ negative), the first term in x in (3.218) is nega-
tive. Also, cosH is positive as H < 90◦ . Then we have to take the ‘+’ sign in front
of the second term, as x = sin φ has to be positive. In this, the krānti and the āśāgrā
are both southerly and difference of the magnitudes of the two terms is to be taken.
