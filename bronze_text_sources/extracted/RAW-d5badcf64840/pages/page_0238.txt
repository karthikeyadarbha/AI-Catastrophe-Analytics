     When the śaṅkvagrā, which changes continuously, becomes equal to the udagarkāgrā,
     then the Sun reaches the prime vertical.

     It is only for this to happen [that is, for the Sun to be on prime vertical] that it is stated that
     the declination has to be north and has to be less than the latitude of the place. [Further,] the
     condition on the declination and latitude is the same as that on the śaṅkvagrā and arkāgrā.

   While the first half of the above verse is straightforward, the second half needs
explanation. For this, let us consider the spherical triangle PZS′ in Fig. 3.22. When
the Sun is not on the prime vertical—as shown at S′ in the figure—the angle PẐS′ 6=
90. Let us denote this angle by ψ and the zenith distance by z. That is, PẐS′ = ψ
and ZS′ = z. Now using the cosine formula we have

                                                      sin δ = cosz sin φ + sin z cos φ cos ψ
                                                     sin δ

This can be written as
                                                       arkāgrā = śaṅkvagrā + X .                                                                  (3.132)
In the above expression, since sin z is always positive, the quantity X is positive only
when ψ < 90◦ . In otherwords, the śaṅkvagrā is less than the arkāgrā only till the
time when the Sun reaches the prime vertical from its rising.
