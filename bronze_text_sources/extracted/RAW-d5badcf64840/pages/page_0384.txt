   The determination of the dr.kkarn.a of the Moon is a little more complicated than
that of the Sun. We explain this with the help of Figs 5.15 and 5.16. In Fig. 5.15, the
point V ′ is the point of intersection of the vertical through the vitribhalagna and the
circle parallel to the ecliptic passing through the Moon. Hence

                                      VV ′ = AM = −βt ,                                       (5.84)

is the magnitude of the true latitude of the Moon, called the viks.epa. The ‘−’ sign in
the above equation indicates that the viks.epa is southwards (as shown in the figure).
Here an intermediate quantity called the nati (nm ) is introduced, which is not to be
confused with the parallax in latitude of the Moon. It is defined to be the sum of the
dr.kks.epa and the viks.epa:

                              nm = ZV ′ = ZV − βt = zv ± |βt |,                               (5.85)

where the sign should be ‘+’ if both have the same direction and ‘−’ if they have
opposite directions. The term paraśaṅku is defined in verse 39a as

                         paraśaṅku = R sin(90 − nm) = R cosnm .                             (5.86)
