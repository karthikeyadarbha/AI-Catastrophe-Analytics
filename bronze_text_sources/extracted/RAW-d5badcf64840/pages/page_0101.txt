    As already explained, in Indian astronomical and mathematical works the cir-
cumference of a circle is taken to be 360◦ = 21600′. Therefore the length of the
arc corresponding to each quadrant will be 5400′. This length is divided into 24
equal segments, each segment corresponding to 225′ . In Fig. 2.2, the points Pi (i =
1, 2, . . . , 24) represent the end points of the 24 segments represented by the arcs
Pi−1 Pi . The set of jyās, Ji = Pi Ni , (i = 1, 2, . . . , 24) corresponding to the 24 cāpas
P0 Pi , are explicitly stated in many texts such as Āryabhat.ı̄ya and Sūryasiddhānta.4
A method for obtaining more accurate values of these tabulated jyās will be pre-
sented in the next verse.
