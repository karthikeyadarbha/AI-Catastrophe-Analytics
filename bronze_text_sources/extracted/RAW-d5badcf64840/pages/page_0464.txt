Fig. 8.4b The exact angular separation between the Sun and the Moon using coordinate geometry.


   In Fig. 8.4b, the ecliptic is taken to be the x–y plane. Further, the x-axis is taken
to be along the direction of the Sun. The line OM, drawn at an angle θ with respect
to the x-axis, is taken to be the direction representing the longitude of the Moon.
The actual positions of the Sun and the Moon as seen by an observer are off the
plane owing to parallax. Their positions are represented by the points S′ and M ′ . For
convenience both are taken to be lying above the plane (+ve z-axis). Taking ηs and
ηm to be the parallactic shifts in latitude of the Sun and the Moon, their coordinates
are given by

                              S′ = (cos ηs , 0, sin ηs )
                  and         M ′ = (cos ηm cos θ , cos ηm sin θ , sin ηm ).           (8.53)

Therefore, the distance between them is given by
