      The true position of the Earth’s shadow is said to be the sum of the true position of the
      Sun and a half-circle (180 degrees). Having determined the position of the Moon and the
      [Earth’s] shadow either at sunrise or at sunset, whichever is closer [to the conjunction],
      the time of their conjunction may be determined. If [the longitude of] the Moon is greater
      then the conjunction is over, and if it is less then it is yet to occur. Their difference [in
      longitude] multiplied by 60 and divided by their difference in daily motion [gives] the time
      for conjunction (the yogakāla) expressed in ghat.ı̄s etc. that has already elapsed or is yet to
      elapse respectively.
