It can be seen that (3.178) is equivalent to (3.170) and (3.172). When the declination
is north (δ positive), | sin δ | = sin δ . In that case, when X > Y , both the solutions
for cosz in (3.178) are positive and z < 90◦ . These correspond to the situation with
the Sun S to the north or south of the prime vertical with the same value of the
āśāgrā, R sin a, but with different values of the azimuth A = 90◦ ± a. When X < Y ,
the second solution for cos z is negative or z > 90◦ , and there is no śaṅku as the Sun
is below the horizon. Also, when R sin δ > K, the solutions for cos z are complex
and there is no śaṅku.
     When the declination is south (δ negative), | sin δ | = − sin δ , the first term in
(3.178) becomes negative, and a physical solution for cos z (positive value) is possi-
ble only when the +ve sign is taken in the second term and Y > X.
