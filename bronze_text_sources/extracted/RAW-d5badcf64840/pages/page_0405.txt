In the Fig. 6.1, Z represents the zenith, P the north celestial pole, Γ the vernal
equinox and N the ascending node of the Moon’s orbit. The Sun is at S whose
declination |δs | = SF. M1 , M2 , M3 and M4 correspond to different positions of the
Moon, which lie in four different quadrants, at which

                                       |δm | = M1 A = M2 B = M3C = M4 D = |δs |.                                                                          (6.2)

Out of these four positions of the Moon—since |δs | is increasing at S—only M2
and M4 correspond to vyatı̄pāta, as |δM | is decreasing only at these two positions.
Moreover, it may be noted that when the Moon is at M2 , the ayanas of the Sun
and the Moon are different but lie in the same hemisphere. Hence it is an instance
of lāt.a-vyatı̄pāta. On the other hand, when the Moon is at M4 , the ayanas of the
Sun and the Moon are the same (both are northerly). Hence this is an example of
vaidhr.ta-vyatı̄pāta.
