We explain the concept of mahāśaṅku and mahācchāyā with the help of Fig. 3.12.
Here, S is the Sun and F is the foot of perpendicular drawn from the Sun to the
horizon. The angle OX̂Y = F ŜO = z is the zenith distance of the Sun and OŶ X =
F ÔS = a = 90 − z is the altitude of the Sun. The triangles OXY and FSO are similar.
In the triangle OXY , OX represents the usual śaṅku of 12 units in height and OY is its
shadow, referred to as the chāyā. Since OXY and FSO are similar, SF (= R cos z) and
FO(= R sin z) are referred to as the mahāśaṅku and the mahācchāyā respectively.
