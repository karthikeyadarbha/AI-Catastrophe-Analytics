   Suppose at a particular instant the Sun is in conjunction with the Moon (M1 in
Fig. 1.3). This instant is taken to be the ending moment of the amāvāsyā, or the new
Moon day. As the Moon’s angular velocity is much greater than that of the Sun,
the angular separation between them keeps increasing. When it becomes exactly
12◦ , that corresponds to the ending moment of the first tithi, namely the pratipad.
Similarly, when the angular separation becomes exactly 24◦ , it corresponds to the

When the angular separation becomes exactly 180◦ , the Moon (M2 in Fig. 1.3) will
be in opposition to the Sun and it corresponds to the ending moment of the pūrva-
paks.a, or the first half of the lunar month. The first fortnight consists of 15 tithis.
It is also referred to as the śukla-paks.a (white fortnight) or the sita-paks.a (bright
fortnight)24 in view of the fact that the brightness of the Moon keeps increasing
