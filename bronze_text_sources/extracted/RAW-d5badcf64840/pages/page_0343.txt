The rationale behind this may be understood with the help of Fig. 4.16b. Here O is
the centre of the shadow. X and X ′ are the centres of the Moon at the beginning of
the eclipse and at a time t later. At the beginning of the eclipse,

                                                     samparkārdha = OA + AX.                              (4.92)

This is the separation between the discs at the first contact. Hence the grāsa = 0. At
a time t later, when the Moon’s centre is at X ′ ,

                                                    samparkārdha = OA′ + X ′Y.                            (4.93)

At this instant,
                                                bimbāntara = OX ′ = OY + Y X ′ .                          (4.94)
Therefore

                                             grāsa = (OA′ + X ′Y ) − (OY +Y X ′ )
                                                          = OA′ − OY
                                                          = A′Y.                                           (4.95)

From the figure it is clear that A′Y is indeed the portion of the Moon which is
obscured, thereby showing that the expression given by (4.91) is exact.

      Because of its brightness, even if one-sixteenth of the Moon is obscured it will not be no-

   Here it is stated that a lunar eclipse would be visible only if more than one-
sixteenth of the Moon is obscured. Similarly it is said that a solar eclipse will be
noticeable only if more than 3′ of the solar disc—which is about one-tenth of the
solar disc—is obscured. These seem to be empirical criteria.
