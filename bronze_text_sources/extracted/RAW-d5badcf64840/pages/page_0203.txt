Duration of risings of rāśis at the equator

Let δ1 , δ2 and δ3 be the declinations of the Sun, when λ = 30◦ , 60◦ and 90◦ re-
spectively. The corresponding right ascensions α1 , α2 and α3 can be obtained using
(3.39). This expression, which gives the right ascension in angular measure, may
be conveniently expressed in other measures too. The relation between the different
measures is given by

                   90◦ = 6 hours = 15 ghat.ikās = 5400 prān.as.                                 (3.40)

Since the right ascensions are known at the end of each of the first three rāśis, the
durations of rising for the first three rāśis, T1 , T2 and T3 , for an observer on the
equator, are obtained using the relations

                    T1 = α1 ;            T2 = α2 − α1 ;                 T3 = α3 − α2 .            (3.41)

The symmetry of the problem clearly suggests that it would be enough to compute
the rising time of the first three rāśis to know the rising time of other rāśis too. Thus
the rising times of other sets of three rāśis have necessarily to be equal to those of
the first set in either the direct order or in reversed order. If T4 , T5 and T6 represent
the rising times of the second set of three rāśis, then they are given by,

                          T4 = T3 ;                T5 = T2 ;            T6 = T1 .                 (3.42)

The relation between the rising times of the second set of 6 rāśis and those of the
first set is given by
                         T6+i = Ti ;     (i = 1, 2, . . . , 6).                (3.43)
