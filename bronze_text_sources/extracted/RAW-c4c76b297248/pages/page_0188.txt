When Parasurama’s first colonists found Kerala uninhabitable and un-improvable, they
abandoned it and returned to their old country. During the time of their absence the
Nagas (serpents) of the lower world, called in popular language Nagalokam or Patala,
took possession of the newly reclaimed land and settled there. The colonists returning
found that the serpents had unsurped their lands, upon which a fight ensued, and
ParaSurama arbitrated between his colonists and the Nagas, with the result that these

These accounts provide the essential features of Serpent worship apparent to an external
observer - it is nothing more than feeding the Cobra or propitiating it outwardly but the
real facts are different. The most famous of the Serpent or Nagaraja shrine in Kerala is
Mannarasala, where the deity worshipped is in fact Naga-vestita । 1108 or the Phallus
adorned with the Serpent, which are none other than the symbols of Mila naksatra and
the nodes of moon Rahu & Sikhi. Both are thus the personifications of time conceived as
Maladhara Rahu-Sikhi Cakra. The Tantric conception of the Serpent is further apparent in
the description of Kundalini as a Sarpa, which remains coiled in the Maladharam.

The major festival days of the temple are of Ayilyam naksatra - the famed constellation of
the Nagas occupying 106°40’ to 120° of the Rasgicakra. Sivaratri and paricami tithi are
also considered important. Ayilyam also receives the appellation Karkétaka in mythology,
who is supposed to have merged with Siva in the Mahakala-vana. The assimilation of the
Siva and the Naga cults can also be inferred from Varahapurana [215:73-74], which
states that at a place called Milaksetra, the serpent Vasuki stands at the gate of Siva
along with many of his attendants

Apparently, at present the custodians of the Serpent temples of Kerala are Nambitiri
Brahmins but the matrilineal succession and the dominant status of the priestess
suggests the temples as remnants of the Nayar worship of the Serpents / Tantrik
tradition. According to lyengar, Nambitiris were the Nagas or Nayars who first subscribed
to the Vedic cult introduced by Paragurama in Kerala and as such it is likely that the
custodians of serpent temples may be the Nayar converts to the Vaidika tradition who
have not forsaken their Serpent gods
