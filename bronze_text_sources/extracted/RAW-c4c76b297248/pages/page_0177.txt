It is in Tantra we find a Universal religion and a casteless society where men and woman
pursue spiritual bliss on an equal footing:

Mahanirvana Tantra: Chapter XIV:

Candalam yavanam nicam matva striyamavajnaya |
Kaulam na kuryyadyah kaulah s6fdham6 yantyadhdgatim ॥ 187 Il

“Kaula, who refuses initiation to Candala or Yavana considering them as inferior or
denying initiation for a woman out of contempt, falls from grace and reduces himself to an
object of contempt”.

Ye ye varnah ksitou santi yadyaddharmamupasritah |
Kaula bhavantaste pasourmukta yanti param padam ॥ 189 Il

“All Varnas (castes) on the earth, irrespective of the religion they practice can break
themselves free of the bond of Karma by becoming a Kaula”.

We see here the true spirituality that allows only one caste one religion and one God and
similar conceptions are not traceable in the Vedic literature. In this context we must
remember that Adi Safkara himself had been an adherent to the Brahmnic classification
of ‘Man the divine’ into four different castes - Caturvarnyam - and as per legends it was
Siva assuming the form of a Candala, who made him realize the purity of soul in all
creatures. This incident is illustrative of the lacunae the Vedic thought had in terms of true
Spirituality and universal appeal and quest for Truth that we see in the Upanisads is the
contributiuon of Tantra — as for example, the Upanisads may be Vedic interpretation of
the Tantric precepts.
