e Technical objections

(i) “The ancient epochs of Maitrayani samhita (1900 BC) and Vedariga Jyotisa (1450 BC)
use winter solstice as the time of starting the year while the Siddhantic and Yajurvedic
astronomers put the starting point of Naksatras at vernal equinox. It is also likely that
some ancient astronomers used autumnal equinox as the starting time of the year. In that
case A-Scorpii should have a longitude of 270° or 180° or O°, but not 240° Actually in
those ancient times astronomers did not use degrees to measure angles; however
counting of days might be prevalent. But if one insists on giving A-Scorpii a longitude of
240, then one has to go back to the earlier epoch of 6000 BC when Caitri full moon
marked the beginning of Uttarayana. And in that case A-Scorpii position of the sun wilf
indicate the beginning of Sarad rtu and not autumnal equinox.

That A-Scorpii has a longitude of 240° in the Babylonian system is indicative of the fact
that their stellar (nirayana) Aries started from a point about 7 west of the vernal equinox
of 300 BC, which can be confirmed from the boundary of Aries in the Norton star atlas
The concept of signs (séyana rasis) was introduced by the Babylonians in their
calculations for covenience. But we adopted their nirayana raSis and fitted them into our
system of naksatras, because they agreed with the vernal equinox of the Siddhantic
period’.

(ii) “The author has talked about a Sura-ganana (tropical lunar) and an Asura-ganana
(sidereal solar) without explaining what they actually mean. Particularly it is not clear why
the sun should be associated with the sidereal and moon with the tropical calendar. What
we have for a very long time is the luni-solar calendar, which takes into account the
positions of both the sun and the moon. Actually a useful calendar has to be Rtu-
samvatsara 1.6., tropical in nature. But there is a very smail difference in the lengths of the
tropical and sidereal years. So a sidereal calendar based on a particular fiduciary
naksatra can serve the purpose for about 1000 years. But one has to change the
fiduciary naksatra by one step after about 1000 years. It is evident that this was the
method followed by our ancient Rsis, because due to ayanacalana they changed the year
beginning at winter solstice from Caitri full moon to Phalguni full moon and then to Maghi
full moon and still later to Magha S (1). Similarly, they changed the enumeration of the
naksatras from Krttika as first naksatra to ASvini as the first naksatra when the vernal
equinox shifted backwards due to precession’. .
