e Amanta and Pirnimanta Months

Months reckoned respectively between the new moons and full moons are known as
Amanta and Pirnimanta. The term Pournamasi means the night on which month
becomes piirna or complete and it implies the Pirnimanta reckoning in force. Dikshit has
quoted Taittiriya Samhita 7.5.6.1 to illustrate the prevalence of both the modes of
reckoning i.e., months ending both on Amavasya and Pirnima. Different verses suggest
a mix-up of both the methods — in some verses the dark half is mentioned first suggesting
the Parnimanta reckoning while the Taittir'ya Brahmana while enumerating the days
gives the names of the days in the light half first followed by dark half suggesting the
Amanta tradition. Use of the terms 0५४8 and apara paksas for Sukla and Krsna paksas
respectively in the Taittirlya Brahmana also indicate the prevalence of amanta reckoning.

(c) Use of ‘Tithi’

Nowhere in the Vedic literature could Dikshit trace the use of the word ‘tithi’ in the sense
of the 30 units of Moon’s elongation. Amavasya, pirnima and astaka have been
interpreted as referring to the respective nights rather than the tithis as we have come to
know in later times. Dikshit has noted that the term ‘Pancadasi’ has occurred in the sense
of ‘15™ in Taittirlya Brahmana and as such the use of ‘Pratipad’, ‘Dvitiya’ etc., to denote
the respective nights of the lunation can be inferred. In the context of the juni-solar
