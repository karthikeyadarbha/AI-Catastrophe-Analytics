— Arani Mathanam and Amrta mathanam are analogus events conceived in mythology.
All ‘devas’ are considered as Agni as Agni marks the beginning of Devayanam.

— Ramayana too is based on the astronomical epoch of 4137 BC when Nirrtya Ravana
(personification of Mila) took possession of the city on the equator, Lanka (autumnal
equinox).

Reflected in the above symbolism of Agni are the efforts by an ancient civilization to
regulate their life in tune with the nature with the help of astronomical knowledge derived
from observations running over perhaps thousands of years. Many of the Hindu
celebrations can therefore be traced to the astronomical phenomena such as year
beginnings, cardinal points (equinoxes and solstices) moon’s phases such as new moon
full moon and the quarters or the luni-solar phenomena etc. We shall revert to this aspect
further in the discussion on Uttarayana and Daksinayana, after a brief look at few other
features of the Hindu way of life.
