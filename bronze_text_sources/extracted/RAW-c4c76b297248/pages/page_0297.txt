B-Tauri (known as Agni) represented the torch that Arjuna held and it heliacally rose
along with Aurigae lying on the galactic equator. Aurigae lying in the Milky Way
represented Citraratha and his company of nymphs engaged in water sports. The
confrontation occurs during their journey to meet Yajhasena, Paricali born of the sacrifice
by Drupada — one of the Twins that emerged from the Yajria. Equinoctial years often
received the appellation of Prajapati or Yajnia in Vedic times and the above story refers to
the twins arising out of Yajna, obviously the author of the allegory had in his mind the
zodical sign Gemini and the epoch is around 4137 BC when the equinox marked the
boundary of Taurus and Gemini.

Citraratha was located at Sdémasrayayanam - an allegorical reference to the star
Mrgagiras also known as Soma, the A - Orionis. Citraratha is referred to as Angaraparna
as Agni [6-Tauri] forms a part of Aurigae. Role of Agni is further apparent in the conflict:

— Arjuna defends himself with the torch [Agni] he had.

— Arjuna destroys the chariot with Agneyastram - born of Brhaspati and Bharadvaja -
given to him by Drénacarya.

— Defeated Citraratha forsakes his name “Afgaraparna’.

— Astronomically when the star Agni was rising B- Leonis or Uttara Phalguni that
represents Arjuna was at the nadir and at the time of sunset when Aurigae sets Arjuni
occupies mid-heaven.
