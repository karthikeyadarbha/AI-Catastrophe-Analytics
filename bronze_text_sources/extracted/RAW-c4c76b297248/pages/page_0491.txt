Concepts of Astronomy

Saptarsis or Ursa Major

Details of the seven bright stars that represent the Saptarsis have been already given in
the section on Vedic astronomy. Mizar or Vasistha is a double having a faint companion
Alcor, known in India as Arundhati. A line drawn through © and 3 meets the Polaris and
these stars are therefore called pointers. In view of this fact it was wrongly construed that
the constellation itself has a movement like the celestial pole.

Hydra, Crater and Corvus

Hydra, the water snake, Crater - the Cup and Corvus - the Crow can be found on the
south of Virgo and Leo. The Solsticial colure had passed through the head of Hydrae
around BC 1000. ० - Hydrae, the brightest star (u = 1.98) is at 122°42’ / (-) 22°23’ and the
constellation figures prominently in the initial chapters of Mahabharata where we find the
stories of Pitrs and Serpents. North of Hydra and south of Zavijava (6 - Virginis) is the
Crater made of four stars and moving further east we find the Crow as a group of five.
These five stars in India had the name Hasta (x - Corvi at 168°52’ / (~) 12°11’) and was
also the Gandiva of Arjuna (U. Phalguni).

Southern Cross and Centarus

South of Hydrae in the Milky Way are the bright constellations Crux and Centarus. The
four principal stars [0 - Crucis or Acrux, 3, y and 4] of Crux forms a cross. Crux also
contains the dark nebula called Coal Sack surrounded by the brighter Milky Way. Certain
scholars have attempted to trace the legend of Tri$ariku and Visvamitra to these groups.
Crux is now wholly invisible beyond 35° N and in the southern hemisphere it is
circumpolar in regions beyond 35 5, very much like the Great Bear or Saptarsis in the
North. During ancient times such as 4000 - 3000 BC it was visible even from 50°N. The
axis of Crux points toward the south celestial pole and a-Crucis or Acrux is close to the
South Pole. It is this proximity to the South Pole that probably created the myth of
Vi$vamitra attempting to create a new heaven for Tri$ahku in opposition to the wishes of
Vasistha, Mizar of the Great Bear.

Libra and Scorpio

This is a constellation of faint stars; © - Librae [20030 / 0020] or ViSakha (Radha) is on
the ecliptic and forms a triangle with 6 and 3 just outside the Milky Way. Straddled to
Libra on the southeast in the Milky Way are the stars of Scorpio led by Antares [a-Scorpii:
22511 / (-) 04°34'](Jyestha Rohint). Antares, a red super giant that rivals Mars in color is
the most popular character of the Vedas — Indra, the genesis of the respective hymns
may belong to the epoch when the solsticial colure had been passing through Antares. B -
Scorpii (Anuradha) lies one degree south of the ecliptic at 21 8°36’. In the solar month of
Sagittarius, Scorpio can be seen an hour before sunrise on the east, a little towards
south.
