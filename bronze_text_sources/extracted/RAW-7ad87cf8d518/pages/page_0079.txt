Ecliptic
One half of the ecliptic, rumning from the beginning
of the sign Aries to the end of the sign Virgo, lies obli-
quely inclined (to the equator) northwards. The
remaining half (of the ecliptic) running from the be-
ginning of the sign Libra to the end of the sign Pisces,
lies (equally inclined to the equator) southwards. (1)

The Nodes of the star-planets (Mars, Mercury,
Jupiter, Venus and Saturn) and of the Moon incessantly
move on the ecliptic. So also does the Sun. From the
Sun, at a distance of half a circle, moves thereon the
Shadow of the Earth. (2)

The Moon moves to the north and to the south of
the ecliptic (respectively) from its (ascending and
descending) Nodes. So also do the planets Mars,
Jupiter and Saturn. Similar is also the motion of the
fighroccas of Mercury and Venus. (3). (KSS)

Sun’s Amplitude, etc.
Sine Amplitude (arkagrd) is the distance in the shape
of the sine of the point from where the Sun touches the

horizon (i.c., points of rising and setting) to the east-
west linet (14a)

The Astodaya-siitra, or the line of rising and setting,
is the east-west Jine joining the two points (of rising and
setting, respectively). (147)

Sine Declination (Krantijya) is the sine of the (angular)
distance between the point where the Sun touches the
Unmandala and the east or west point (Svastika). Let
this be the bhuwd (altitude). Let the radius of-the
diurnal circle on which the Sun is, viz., cos. declination
(dyu-dala) be the kofi (base) ; the radius of the sphere will
(then) be the hypotenuse (karna) of the right-angled
triangle. (15-16a)

The circle along which the position of the Sun moves
is called the Diurnal circle (dyuvrita). (167)

The sine of the (arc of the) diurnal circle between the
horizon and the Unmandala is called Ksitijya. Let it be
the bhujd. Let sine declination be the kofi here. Then
sine amplitude will be hypotenuse; these three make a
(right-angled) triangle. (17). (KVS)
