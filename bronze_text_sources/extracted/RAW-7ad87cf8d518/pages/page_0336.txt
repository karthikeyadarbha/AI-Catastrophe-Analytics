Sine Drggati

The root of the square of the Drgjya (t.e., Sine Zenith

distance of the planet, 7.e., Sin. ZS) minus the square of
Sine Drkksepa (Sin. ZD) is called Sine Drggati (Sin. DS).
Since the difference in longitude due to the difference
of the position of the observer at the centre and at the
surface of the Earth (drgbhedotiha gati) is got from this,
it is called Drggatijya.1 (20)

That (96) from which (the portion of) the latitude
caused by the Drgbheda (i.e., the difference of view from
the surface and the centre of the Earth) is obtained is
called the कद for the Drkksepa. The difference of view
is in accordance with the Chaya (Sine of the Zenith
distance). Hence, here, the Drkksepajya is the same as
Drgjya (Sine Zenith distance) of the Nonagesimal. (21)

The Drgbheda is zero at the zenith. It is equal to
the radius of the Earth at the horizon, in its own
orbit (SiSi). In between, it is (SS’), proportionate (to
the Sine of the Zenith distance, 2 ZCS). (22)

In the case of the altitudes of a planet (5 and S’) in
the two circles with the observer (E) and the centre of the
the Earth (C) (respectively, as centres), the difference
(of the centres) is the Drgbheda (SS’). Viewed from the
observer as centre, its direction is downwards. (23)
