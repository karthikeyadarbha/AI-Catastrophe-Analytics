Local latitude and colatitude from Eq. shadow

The radius severally multiplied by the equinoctial
midday shadow and 12 (gnomon), and divided by
the hypotenuse of the shadow, gives, respectively, the R
sine of the latitude of the place and the R sine of the
colatitude. Their arcs, divided by 60, give, respectively,
in degrees, (the latitude and the colatitude). The
latitude is. always to the south. The direction of the
declination of the Sun depends on its (i.e., the Sun’s)
position. (5). (BC)

Sine co-latitude and day-diameter

Square the. sine of Jatitude and deduct the result
from the square of the radius. Its square root is the
‘sine of co-latitude’, (its arc being the ‘co-latitude’).
Square the sine of declination, deduct it from the square
of the radius and. find its 100६. ` Twice the result is the
‘day-diameter’. (23)

The sines of the declinations of the points of the
ecliptic ending Aries, Taurus and Gemini are 24’ 24”,
42’ 15” and 48’ 48’. (24)

The respective day-diameters are, in terms of minutes:
200 + 35, 200 - 24 and 2004 19, with 40” and
15" added to the second and third, i.e., the day-
diameters are 235’, 204’ 40” and 219 15’’.1 (25).

Samamandala-Saiku from Eq. shadow

When the hypotenuse of the equinoctial midday
shadow is multiplied by the R sine of the declination of
the Sun and the product divided severally by the gnomon
(of 12 angulas) and by the equinoctial midday shadow,
the results are, respectively, R sine of the amplitude
of the Sun or R sine of the agrd or agrajyd, and R sine
altitude of the Sun when on the prime vertical or sama-
fanku. The square root of the sum of the squares of the
R sine agrd and samasanku is the hypotenuse or taddhrtz.
(6). (BC)

Hour angle

That part of the sky which is to the east (of the
observer’s meridian) is the eastern hemisphere and the
part to the west is the western hemisphere. So the
wise say.
