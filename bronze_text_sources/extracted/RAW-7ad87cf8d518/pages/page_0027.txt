Main conceptual aspects of Indian astronomy

As already noted, the Indian astronomers conceived of a huge
period of 43,20,000 years called a mahdyuga or caturyuga and even a
period 1000 times longer, i.e., 43,20,000,000 years called a Kalpa.
In the Saura and the Brahma schools, the kalpa is regarded as being
equivalent to 14 manvantaras, a manvantara being equal to 71 maha- '
yugas or 306,720,000 years to which must be added 15 sandhis, each.
sand being 17,28,000 years, which again is equal to the period of
Krtayuga. According to the Ardharatrika system of Aryabhata, the
kalpa consists of 4,354,560,000 years but equivalent to 14 manvaniaras,
each manvantara being composed of 72 mahayugas. Thus the kalpa
consists of (14x 72) 1008 makdyugas. In this system also, a mahdyuga
is equal to 43,20,000 years. However, here, the four ages, namely,
Krta, Treta, Dvépara and Kali, are regarded as being of equal duration
(each having 1,080,000 years), unlike in the Saura and Brahma, where
they are in the ratio of 4:3:2:1 with sandhis.
