(For the devas, 1.6. at the North pole, the zenith is the
north celestial pole, any visible semi-great-circle passing
through the zenith being a meridian on the earth.) It
is 3 Signs’ distance, i.e. 90°, from the zenith to the

horizon. This is to be divided into 90 parts, so that
each division isa degree. (14)

These degree-divisions in the sky are zeniths of
corresponding degrees of latitudes on the Earth along
any meridian, and the distance between each degree of
latitude is 8 8/9 yoyanas. (15)

Thus for 90°, there are 800 yojanas, as calculated.
When an observer on any meridian sees the Sun rising,
it is midday on the meridian 800 yojanas (east) of
him. (16)

The midday of Lanka is the same as that at Ujjain,
which is north of Lanka on the same longitude. But
their day-time durations are different, except when the
Sun is on the equator. (17). (TSK)

At any latitude, the equatorial Sun is bent so many
degrees south at midday, as the north pole is raised
from the north point of the horizon. (20)

Going north from Ujjain, 373 1/3 yojanas, the stellar
sphere, (marked by the 27 asterisms cf the ecliptic
rising in order), becomes discontmuous, (i.e. the order
in the rising is disrupted.)! (21)

At that latitude, the Sun can be visible even
throughout a day. North of this place, the Sun may
not set more than one day, until at the North pole it
will not set for six months at a stretch. (22)
