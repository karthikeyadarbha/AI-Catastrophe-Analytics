R sines of the bhuja and kop

After converting the bhujdé and the other (i.e., the koft)
into minutes of arc and dividing by 225, (in each case),
take (the sum of) as many R sine-differences as the
quotient. Then multiply the remainder (in each case)
by the current (i.e., next) R sine-difference and divide
by 225 and add the result (to the corresponding sum of
the R sine-differences obtained above). (The sums
thus obtained are the R sines of the bhujé and the kofi).
(2b-3a)

Bhujaphala and kotipala

They (ie., the R sines of the bhyja and the koft)
multiplied by the (planet’s tabulated) epicycle should
be divided by 80: the results are (known as) bhujaphala
and kotiphala. (3b)

Bhujaphala correction

The bhujaphala is additive or subtractive according as
the (mean) anomaly is in the half-orbit commencing
with the sign Libra or in that commencing with the

sign Aries. (4a)

Bhujantara correction

So also is applied (the bhujaniara correction) which is
obtained by multiplying the (mean daily) motion of the
planet by the (Sun’s) bhujaphala and. dividing by the
number of minutes of arc in a circle (i.e., 21600). (4b)

One-sixth of the (Sun’s) bhujdphala is, in seconds of
arc, (the bhujantara correction) for the Sun; that for the
Moon is obtained in minutes of arc etc. by multiplying
(the Sun’s bhujaphala) by 3 and dividing by 82. (5)

True distances of Sun and Moon

Increase or diminish the radius by the (Sun’s)
kotiphala (according as the mean Sun is) in the half-orbit
commencing with the anomalistic Sign Capricorn or in
that commencing with Cancer. The square root of the
sum of the squares of that and the (Sun’s) bahuphala is
the (first approximation to the Sun’s) distance. (Several-
ly) multiply that by the (Sun’s) bahuphala and kotiphala
and divide (each product) by the radius: (the results
are again the Sun’s bahuphala and kofiphala). (Making
use of them calculate the Sun’s distance afresh: thus is
obtained the second approximation to the Sun’s distance.
(Repeat this process again and again and thus) by the
method of successive approximations, obtain the nearest
approximation to the Sun’s (true) distance. For the
Moon, too, this is to be regarded as the method for
finding the nearest approximation to the true distance.
(6-7).
